Before making [Infinite Roads](../infinite-roads/), I wanted to understand why one stickman survival video, *Bunker Survival: 1 Year* by Galactic Highway, had reached about 1.6 million views on a channel with only about 55,000 subscribers. So I took it apart.

## What I did

I analysed the same video in two different ways:

1. **An AI watched it** (Gemini) and described the narration, the music, the sound effects, the visuals, the editing and the structure.
2. **A separate frame-by-frame analysis** looked at what was actually on screen.

## Where they disagreed

The two analyses contradicted each other about the look. The AI described dark, cinematic concept art. The frame analysis showed flat vector line-art stills, hard cuts, and a small orange-cap character.

I decided to trust the frames for the visuals and the AI for the audio and structure. Then I checked the audio myself, by ear, and found out the AI was **wrong there too**: the video has no background music at all. The AI's music notes were simply wrong.

## What I took from it

- **The structure** above: a hook, then the rules of survival, then the grind, then things failing, then something unexplained.
- **Hyper-specific timelines** (Day 1, Month 3, Month 6) with practical details that make it feel real.
- **A cliffhanger at the end,** built around an unknown knock.

My channel's look is its own: a fixed doodle stickman in a bold marker style. And by October, six other channels had copied the same format, so knocks and radio voices were already cliches, which is why I now rotate my cliffhangers.

## The lesson

Don't take one AI's word for something, even when it sounds confident and detailed. Check it against a second source, and where you can, against your own eyes and ears.
